How a round works

  1. 1
    Watch it work.It moves once, with its own sound, then comes apart.
  2. 2
    Place, then confirm.Drag each part into place and turn the turned one; every 15 s the built object flashes up. Wrong parts cost +3 s.
  3. 3
    Leave the decoy out.A look-alike from another object, or a fake a friend forged live with Lux3D.
  4. 4
    Make it work.On 11 objects, work it by hand to stop the clock.
  5. 5
    Find the hinge.On the rest that swing or turn, tap where it pivots.

Play together

Race friends by link

Share a room link. Everyone gets the same three objects and starts each one together, as the parts arrive. Progress is live.

The first finisher starts a 30-second countdown. Lowest total time wins. See the podium, then rematch.

Climb six levels alone

Level 1 is two-part objects; Level 6 goes up to 13 parts. Rebuild 3 objects of a level to open the next. Players join with a name; the board ranks them by level.

Each round races a real earlier run, when one exists. None are made up. A challenge link sends a friend your run, with the same decoy.

How Lux3D makes every object

Text-to-3D

Lux3D G1-Turbo turns a text prompt into a textured .glb model.

Part-split

Lux3D separates the model into real components, in the model's own frame.

Articulation

Lux3D returns a .urdf: which parts move, on which axis, and how far.

Our rig step

spike/rig.py matches each split part to its moving link. It cuts parts that span a joint.

58objects

337split parts

65moving joints

Parts include bases and merged entries. Joints exclude fixed joints and include the one joint we set by hand (the radio's knob).

Sources: app/public/objects/index.json / each object's rig.json / spike/4

The rebuilt object moves

One mesh, both outputs

Part-split and articulation start from the same mesh. The parts line up with the movement when you rebuild the object.

Cut across the joint

A split part can span a joint. Our rig step assigns its triangles to links, so each side moves with its link.

Work it by hand

Your drag drives the real Lux3D joint: its axis, its pivot and its limits. The padlock's key turns only round its own axis.

What we tried and dropped

Waffle ironThe model came out nearly black.
Sewing machine and cameraThe models had brand-like lettering.
Cuckoo clockIt was too intricate to split.
Mailbox and clothes pegPart-split could not separate them.
Wrench, nutcracker, garlic press and pocket knifeThey split into only two parts.
Vernier caliper and office chairThe caliper split into one piece and its screw; the chair's articulation failed twice.

Kept, with corrections

The laptop closes on a corrected Lux3D hinge. The radio's knob moves on a joint we set by hand, and the game says so. We narrowed or reversed seven objects' range and moved the lunch box hinge.

Forge a fake, live

A player describes a fake part. The Worker asks Lux3D for it, the page shrinks its textures, and the friend's tray hides it.

The forge closes with the credits on 19 November. Two fakes made before then stay playable.

Limits

Times come from
each player's page.

Each page reports its own time, and the server rejects runs that don't add up. That is fine among friends. It is not cheat-proof. A name is not an account: another browser is another player.

Objects are made
ahead of play.

Only the forge calls Lux3D while you play, and it closes with the hackathon's credits on 19 November, before judging.
